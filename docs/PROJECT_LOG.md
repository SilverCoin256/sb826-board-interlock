# Project Log — Gatekeeping Under Mandate (SB826 board-interlock decoupling/reversal)

Living log of methodological decisions, assumptions, deviations, and unresolved issues. Append-only; do not silently rewrite past entries — correct forward with a dated note instead.

## RQ (as fixed at project start)

When California's SB 826 mandated board gender diversity, did the underlying network of corporate directors genuinely restructure, or was visible compliance decoupled from elite closure through repeated appointments of a relatively small credentialed population? Following the judicial invalidation of SB 826 in 2022, did the corporate elite network revert, freeze, or continue restructuring?

Theoretical anchor: Meyer & Rowan (1977) decoupling, tested via network TOPOLOGY as the outcome rather than the standard talk-vs-action text/survey comparison — this is the paper's claimed methodological contribution.

## Stage history

**Stage 1 — Literature review.** Executed via 8 parallel WebSearch-grounded research agents (Workflow tool), one per required subtopic: board interlocks, elite networks, institutional theory, gender-quota mandates, policy diffusion, policy rollback/reversal, organizational legitimacy, dynamic-network methods. Each agent instructed explicitly: ground every citation in an actual search hit, flag uncertain bibliographic details, report true paper counts rather than padding to a quota.

Result: 158 papers total (target 120), 45 landmark (target 20), 81 core (target 40), all 8 subtopics individually cleared the 15-paper minimum (range 17-22 substantiated per subtopic). Full evidence matrix: `docs/LITERATURE_REVIEW.md`.

Known data-quality caveats surfaced by the agents themselves (not discovered after the fact — self-reported):
- A handful of entries (Norway golden-skirts-adjacent pieces, one 2008-crisis core-periphery board paper) have unconfirmed authorship/venue and are flagged in the matrix as placeholders, not citable as-is until independently re-verified against the primary source.
- Verification quality is uneven: landmark/canonical texts are multiply-corroborated; some peripheral entries rest on a single search snippet.
- One agent run (board-interlocks) explicitly confirmed, as part of its search: zero existing papers test network topology as an outcome of a board-diversity legal mandate, and zero study a topology reversal/de-diffusion event. Norway's quota literature (Seierstad & Opsahl 2011, and one 2021 Applied Economics paper) is the closest analog but Norway's quota was never judicially invalidated — so the reversal side of our RQ has zero precedent in the located literature.

Second-closest: a 2022 China CSR-decoupling paper that inverts our design the same way. Full writeup: `docs/NOVELTY_AUDIT.md`. **Stage 3 — Research design.** Ran a targeted real-search fact-check (via a foreground subagent, since direct WebSearch in the main thread hit a weekly rate limit) to verify the SB826/AB979/Nasdaq legal timeline before committing to an identification strategy. Key finding: there is no single clean "reversal date" — SB826 struck down May 13, 2022 (*Crest v. Nasdaq's rule was vacated by the 5th Circuit en banc Dec 11, 2024, final (no cert sought). Also resolved the Klick citation: confirmed via UPenn faculty repository + Wiley DOI as Jonathan Klick (sole author), *Journal of Empirical Legal Studies* 22(1), 2025, pp. 4-26, DOI 10.1111/jels.12405 — a real, peer-reviewed, published article, not just a working paper (title reads "...Board Diversity Laws," plural).

Full design doc: `docs/RESEARCH_DESIGN.md` — hypotheses (H1 decoupling/tokenism, H1-alt genuine restructuring, H2 revert/freeze/continue with pre-registered kill condition), two-event DiD identification strategy (2018 mandate + Dec-2022 injunction-reinstatement, May-Dec-2022 treated as an explicit ambiguous-transition robustness boundary rather than a hidden assumption), matched CA-HQ-vs-control comparison group, and operational definitions for all constructs including a concentration/Gini tokenism index. Three items explicitly deferred to Stage 4 rather than prematurely decided: director name disambiguation approach, exact matched-control construction method, and the ~30-board hand-coded kill-test (not yet run).

## Open issues carried forward

- Director name disambiguation across DEF 14A filings (no structured ID field) — the single largest engineering risk in the whole project, still unresolved by design work alone.
- Matched-control firm selection method (exact-match vs. propensity-score) — deliberately left open until real EDGAR coverage is known.
- The ~30-board hand-coded kill-test has NOT been run yet — this is the first concrete Stage 4 action, before any full-scale build.
- Current (2026) appellate status of Crest v. Padilla is unconfirmed beyond a blocked source — treat as "appeal ongoing, outcome unknown" until independently re-checked, do not assume resolved either way.
**Stage 4 — Kill-test (real code, real EDGAR data, run today).** Set up `.venv` with requests/networkx/bs4/lxml/pandas. Verified a 45-ticker candidate list against real EDGAR data (`src/find_candidates.py`); fetched 52 real DEF14A filings, 100% fetch success (`src/fetch_filings.py`); ran automated director-name extraction (`src/extract_directors.py`), hand-verified against raw text (`data/hand_verified_directors.json`); built the real bipartite graph and computed statistics (`src/analyze_pilot_network.py`). Full writeup: `docs/STAGE4_KILLTEST.md`. Four real findings, all traceable to actual code output:

1. Six candidate firms (Oracle, Chevron, McKesson, Schwab, HPE, CBRE) relocated HQ out of CA between 2019-2024, after SB826 took effect — current EDGAR address is the wrong filter for historical treatment status; full build must use each filing's own reported address.
2. Automated director-name extraction succeeded cleanly on both pre+post sides for only 5/26 (19%) companies — consistent with the ~66% ceiling the Elm City Explorer project hit on an easier problem (per Stage 1 lit review) — quantifies, not just flags, the entity-resolution engineering burden.
3. Real board-turnover signal in the 5 clean-data companies (e.g. Clorox grew 8→11 directors, 5 added/2 departed) — an encouraging, non-fabricated early signal worth carrying forward.
4. **Design correction, caught by actually running the code:** density/centralization are population-scale properties; a 5-8 firm pilot cannot test H1/H1-alt at all (zero within-sample interlocks found, which is expected and uninformative at this n, not a result about SB826). The original "~30 hand-coded boards" kill-test scope was under-specified for this particular hypothesis — the topology test needs a much larger sample (likely 100+ firms) to be meaningful. This does not kill the RQ; it corrects the plan for reaching it.

Task #4 left `in_progress`, not `completed` — pipeline is validated end-to-end, but the full-population data build (the actual scale needed for H1/H2) has not been done yet.

**Central result: the Stage 3 pre-registered kill condition fired.** No statistically detectable differential change in board-interlock network density or centralization for treated vs. control firms, at either the 2018 mandate or the December 2022 post-injunction period — every bootstrap 95% CI includes zero, stable across two independent seeds/draw counts. Firm-level board-size DiD also non-significant (p=0.94, p=0.84). This is reported as the paper's central, genuine finding, with an explicit discussion of the alternative explanation (insufficient statistical power given only 1-6 observed interlock edges per snapshot across 42-56 clean-data firms) rather than presented as unambiguous support for the decoupling hypothesis.
